# Joining

How a new volunteer gets from "I'd like to help" to doing something useful. The checklist for the person running it is in [Onboarding checklist](/handbook/onboarders).

> **Draft.** This process, including the response times, is proposed in the November 2026 onboarding review and is not yet adopted by the Executive Director.

## 1. Write to us

Write to **info@neurotechx.org** (the address on [Join the Team](https://neurotechx.org/join-the-team)) or **volunteer@neurotechx.com**. Tell us:

- what you would like to do, or which listed role interests you;
- roughly how many hours a month you can give, and your time zone;
- anything that could be a conflict of interest (see [The contributor agreement](/handbook/agreement)), for example a company you run or an employer in neurotechnology. Having one is normal; we just need to know.

We reply within **three working days**.

## 2. An intro call

Twenty to thirty minutes with the person who will look after you, usually the lead of the project you are joining. By the end of the call you should have:

- a **first task** you can finish in a week or two;
- a **point person** to ask questions;
- a date for the 30-day check-in.

## 3. The contributor agreement

Everyone who works on NeuroTechX itself signs the contributor agreement once, before getting access to anything non-public. It takes a few minutes. [The contributor agreement](/handbook/agreement) explains every clause in plain language.

You do not need to sign it to post in public Slack channels, come to events or open a pull request on a public repository.

## 4. Access

On the day you sign, you get what your first task needs and no more (see [Tools and access](/handbook/tools-and-access)):

- the Slack channels for your project;
- the GitHub repository or team for your project;
- the shared folder for your project;
- a NeuroTechX email address **only** if your role means writing to people outside NeuroTechX on its behalf.

More access comes as your role grows. That is how we keep accounts safe, not a judgement on you.

## 5. Your first weeks

- Read this handbook, especially [Personal information](/handbook/privacy) and [Working together](/handbook/working-together).
- Introduce yourself in Slack.
- Do the first task, and ask your point person when you are stuck. Asking early is better.

## 6. Check-ins

- **After 30 days**: how is it going, is the task right, what next.
- **Every three months after that**: a short message from your project lead.
- If you go quiet for three months we will write once to ask whether you want to continue. If we hear nothing, we remove access, and you are welcome back any time.

If something is not working, tell your point person or the Executive Director.
