# Personal information

NeuroTechX is based in Quebec, so **Quebec's private-sector privacy law** (as amended by "Law 25") applies to the personal information we hold: newsletter subscribers, event registrants, Slack members, applicants, volunteers. Our public promise is the [privacy policy](https://neurotechx.org/privacy). Canada's anti-spam law (CASL) governs who we may email.

## The rules

1.  **Use personal information only for the task it was collected for.** An event's registrant list is for that event.
2.  **Never build lists.** Don't copy names or emails out of Slack, GitHub, LinkedIn, event pages or a mailbox into a spreadsheet or mailing list. People join our mailing lists only by signing up themselves.
3.  **Don't export or download in bulk** unless your task requires it. If you must (for example a registrant list for a venue), keep it in the project's shared folder, not on your own computer or account, and delete it when the event is over.
4.  **Share the minimum.** Send a link to a restricted folder rather than attaching a list.
5.  **Don't paste personal information into outside tools** such as AI assistants, translation sites or survey tools that NeuroTechX has not approved.
6.  **No personal information in public places**: GitHub repositories, public Slack channels, meeting notes that will be shared.
7.  **Requests from people** to see, correct or delete their information go to [info@neurotechx.org](mailto:info@neurotechx.org). Forward them; don't handle them yourself.

## If something goes wrong

A **confidentiality incident** is any access to, use of, or disclosure of personal information that should not have happened, or its loss. Examples: an email with a registrant list sent to the wrong person, a shared folder that was public, a lost laptop with a download on it, a hacked account.

**Tell the Executive Director the same day**, and at the latest within 24 hours, even if you are not sure it counts. Say what happened, what information, how many people, and what you have done so far. Don't try to cover it up or fix it quietly. The law requires us to record every incident and, if there is a risk of serious harm, to notify the people affected and Quebec's privacy commission. We can only do that if we know.
