# Onboarding checklist

For whoever brings a new volunteer in: the Executive Director, a project lead, or a future Volunteer Coordinator.

> **Draft.** Proposed in the November 2026 onboarding review, not yet adopted.

## Principles

- **One owner per newcomer**, from first email to 30-day check-in. If you hand over, say so to the newcomer.
- **Nothing waits on anything else that doesn't need to.** Slack and this handbook don't need the agreement; non-public access does.
- **Least access first**, more as the role grows.
- **Every newcomer is in the tracker.** If it isn't in the tracker, it isn't happening.

## The tracker

A restricted Google Sheet in the Volunteers shared folder, readable by the ED and project leads. One row per person: name, email, project, owner, stage, date of each stage, agreement version and date, access granted, next check-in. No CVs, no notes about the person beyond what onboarding needs.

Stages: *enquiry → call booked → agreed first task → agreement signed → access given → 30-day check-in → active → left*.

## Step by step

**Day 0–3: reply.** From volunteer@ or info@, within three working days:

> Thank you for offering to help. Here is our volunteer handbook: \<link\>. Could you tell me what you would like to work on, about how many hours a month you can give, your time zone, and any possible conflict of interest (for example a company you run)? Then let's book 20 minutes: \<times or booking link\>.

Add the row to the tracker.

**Within 10 working days: the call.** Agenda: what they want to do and learn; what we need; agree one **first task** finishable in one or two weeks; name the **point person**; explain the agreement; book the 30-day check-in.

**Right after the call: the agreement.** Send the current Contributor Acceptance Form. When it comes back: file it in the restricted *Agreements* folder, named `YYYY-MM-DD Surname Firstname CAF vN.pdf`, record date and version in the tracker, and record any conflict disclosed. Never leave the signed copy only in your inbox.

**Same day as the signature: access.**

| Give | When |
|----|----|
| Slack, project channels | Always |
| GitHub team for the project | They will push to NeuroTechX repositories |
| Private repository | Only if the work requires it; each person is a paid seat |
| Shared project folder | Always, shared to their address |
| NeuroTechX email address | Only if they will write to outsiders for NeuroTechX |
| Role mailbox | Only when they take over that role |
| Shared logins | Only through the ED, never by message |

For GitHub, add people to a team rather than to individual repositories. For a NeuroTechX address, ask the ED (it needs a Workspace administrator); the new user turns on 2-Step Verification on day one.

**First week.** Introduce them in Slack. Check they could open everything. Answer questions quickly; the first week decides whether people stay.

**Day 30: check-in.** How is it going, is the role right, what is next. Update the tracker.

**Quarterly.** A short message from the lead. No contact for three months: write once; no answer in two weeks: offboard (see [Stepping back or leaving](/handbook/leaving)).

## Offboarding checklist

- \[ \] Private Slack channels removed
- \[ \] GitHub teams and private repositories removed
- \[ \] Folders unshared; files they owned transferred
- \[ \] Workspace account suspended, forwarded or handed over
- \[ \] Shared passwords they knew changed
- \[ \] Role mailboxes and listings updated (website, Join the Team)
- \[ \] Tracker row set to *left* with date
- \[ \] Thanked, and offered a reference

## Writing a reference

Describe the role, dates, what they did and what came of it. The ED signs references written for NeuroTechX.
